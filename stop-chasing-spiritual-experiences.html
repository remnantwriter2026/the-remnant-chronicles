Stop Chasing Spiritual Experiences

The Danger of Seeking Experiences Above God’s Truth

By Jimey Grant Lafayette, Louisiana Second Watch

Spiritual curiosity can open doors to experiences we may not be prepared to face. Perhaps you have felt drawn to spiritual practices, attended or are curious about attending a spiritual circle, or experimented with things you cannot fully explain. Maybe you tell yourself it is only for fun, insight, or a new experience. Yes, you may have on your own found out and discovered that you have a spiritual gift or are spiritually sensitive, but this does not give you a license to pursue spiritual power or a guarantee that every spiritual encounter will lead to a particular outcome, because spiritual curiosity without the proper guidance of the Holy Spirit can carry serious consequences.

The Bible does not present the spiritual realm as a safe arena for casual experimentation. It warns that spiritual conflict is real and calls us to seek the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob rather than engage with forces of the demonic as well as the unknown. Matthew 11:12 says, “From the days of John the Baptist until now, the kingdom of heaven has been subjected to violence, and violent people have been raiding it and taking it by force.” This verse speaks about the kingdom of heaven and the violent conflict surrounding it;

Scripture portrays demonic oppression and possession as sources of real suffering—not as props for entertainment or stories meant merely to frighten us. Those who dabble in spiritual practices expose themselves to potential spiritual harm. Some people report troubling experiences after exploring such practices. Spiritual ignorance is no reason to treat these experiences and rituals casually, nor should we regard occult practices as shortcuts to hidden knowledge. Scripture gives sobering accounts of suffering associated with demonic forces.

In Mark 9:17–27, a father describes how a spirit constantly threw his son into fire and water, putting the boy’s life in danger. This account shows us the violent and uncontrollable nature of spiritual forces.

Mark 5:1–5 describes a man living among the tombs, crying out and cutting himself with stones. His condition was severe, and Jesus later freed him from the spirits tormenting him (Mark 5:6–15). Once again we see the nature of the spiritual realm

In 1 Kings 18:25–29, the prophets of Baal, in a demonic occultic ritual, were crying out and cutting themselves with swords and spears until blood flowed as they called on their false god. This account shows the brutality, futility, and disturbing distress of seeking help from a false god. Together, these passages should make us cautious: we should not seek spiritual power apart from the truth and protection of God.

In Acts 19:13–16, the seven sons of Sceva tried to invoke the name of Jesus over a man with an evil spirit. The evil spirit answered that it knew Jesus and recognized Paul, but did not know them; and because of that the evil spirit attacked them, and they fled wounded, beaten, and naked. This account warns against treating spiritual authority as something to imitate or invoke without truly knowing Christ.

Stop chasing rituals or experimenting with occult practices in search of control. Do not pursue demons, spiritual power, or supernatural phenomena out of curiosity.

This is not a message of panic. It is a call to wisdom and repentance. The Christian call is not to chase spiritual experiences, but to know Christ. A spiritual thrill may promise discovery, but it cannot give you the lasting truth, freedom, and hope found in him. Do not let spiritual curiosity lead you into harm.

Repent and turn to Christ now and not after one more spiritual experience. Spiritual curiosity cannot save you, and spiritual power outside of Christ cannot free you. Christ alone offers truth, mercy, and lasting hope. Turn from every practice that draws you away from God. Seek Him, trust Him, and walk in the freedom only He can give.
