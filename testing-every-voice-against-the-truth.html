Testing Every Voice Against the Truth

A Biblical Standard for Weighing What We Hear

By Jimey Grant Lafayette, Louisiana Second Watch

As believers in Christ, one of the most vital disciplines we must develop is the wisdom found in 1 John 4:1: “Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God.” Our strength does not rest in human opinion; it rests in God's word. When Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, He did not defeat Satan with personal opinion or worldly research. He overcame temptation with the Word of God. When circumstances make us question our walk with Christ, Scripture is our first defense.

Christians can stumble when debates introduce doubt and confusion. Someone who seems more biblically or doctrinally informed may raise a point we have never heard before, or share something we have not yet studied. Faced with unfamiliar information, we may begin to question our faith—or reject the idea before examining it. True spiritual maturity requires us to slow down, ask honest questions, and evaluate claims against divine revelation rather than reacting in fear or intellectual pride. That is why every Christian should know the Bible and approach difficult questions with humility. Scripture says to trust in God and not lean on our own understanding. John 1:1 begins, “In the beginning was the word, and word was with God, and the word was God”—not with human ideology or speculation.

Testing the spirits means examining what we hear and asking whether it aligns with Scripture. Yet knowing the Bible is not the only part of discernment; we must also consider what spirit is behind a situation, question, or conversation. When Satan tempted Jesus in the wilderness, like in my prior example, satan quoted Psalm 91:11–12, to tempt Jesus to throw Himself down. The Bible also warns that satan disguises himself as an angel of light. A biblical-sounding argument should still be tested by its meaning, context, and purpose.

Scripture must remain our primary standard, while careful research can serve as a secondary tool. The Bible speaks of mysteries of the kingdom—spiritual truths revealed by the Holy Spirit. When people distort sacred text or introduce concepts not explicitly defined in Scripture, we must measure their motives against Christ's character. Unanchored investigation easily allows deceptive philosophies to hijack our faith under the guise of deep knowledge. Some people also discuss entities such as succubi, incubi, and water spirits, which are not named in Scripture. Claims about them require careful evaluation, not automatic acceptance. Research can help us understand where such ideas come from and how they are connected to beliefs about spiritual practices, but it should never outrank God’s Word.

The church in Thyatira offers a sobering example of why believers must test spiritual teaching. In Revelation 2:18–29, Jesus warns the church that it tolerates a teacher He calls “Jezebel.” She claimed to speak for God, but her teaching led believers into sexual immorality and participation in idol worship. Her influence shows that spiritual-sounding claims must be judged by their fruit and faithfulness to Christ.

Paul warns Timothy that “The Spirit clearly says that in later times some will abandon the faith and follow deceiving spirits and things taught by demons” (1 Timothy 4:1 NIV). He describes people who promote falsehood and present human rules as spiritual truth. This warning reminds the church that not every teaching that sounds religious comes from God. Believers must examine what is being taught, compare it with Scripture, and refuse ideas that distort the gospel or draw people away from Christ.

Discernment also shapes how we respond to people who disagree with us. We should neither ridicule sincere questions nor accept every confident explanation as truth. Instead, listen carefully, seek the full context, and compare each claim with the whole counsel of Scripture. Prayer and wise counsel from mature believers can help us recognize when an idea reflects Christ’s character and when it distorts His teaching. This patient approach protects our faith while allowing us to grow in understanding, compassion, and confidence in God’s Word.
