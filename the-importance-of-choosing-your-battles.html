The Importance of Choosing Your Battles

By Jimey Grant Lafayette, Louisiana Second Watch

When people, especially young Christians in the faith, get saved and filled with the Holy Spirit, some of them think that the Holy Spirit means unlimited power, boldness, fire, and zeal. And if not mentored and guided properly, the first thing they wind up doing is learning spiritual warfare, the supernatural, rebuking, and learning about the enemy. And it's because of the grace and mercy of the Most High that they don't get obliterated both spiritually and naturally due to their ignorance of the spiritual realm. Notice when the power of God came upon the men of God in the Old Testament, God was divinely empowering them to fulfill his will and his will alone. The boldness did not come from self.

Scripture warns us about boldness that is not guided by wisdom or obedience to God. King Josiah faithfully led reforms against idolatry and restored worship of the Lord (2 Kings 22–23). But when he went to battle against Pharaoh Neco, despite Neco’s warning that God had commanded him not to interfere, Josiah was fatally wounded (2 Chronicles 35:20–24). His story reminds us that courage alone is not enough; we must seek God’s direction and remain attentive to His will.

When making decisions, we must take into account the strategic vision and leadership of Joshua. When the Israelites approached Jericho, its walls were heavily fortified, and a direct assault seemed impossible. But Joshua sought the Lord, and God gave him a different strategy: rather than attack, the people were to march around the city as instructed. Joshua obeyed, and the walls of Jericho fell (Joshua 6:1–20). The victory came not through conventional warfare, but through trusting God’s direction and moving according to His plan. So in this example we see the option of either fighting or running, but Joshua chose to simply maneuver around.

Joseph’s response to Potiphar’s wife is another example of wisdom. He didn’t linger or try to prove he could withstand temptation—he fled (Genesis 39:7–12). Scripture tells us to “flee youthful passions” (2 Timothy 2:22), and Joseph’s decisive action helped him remain faithful to God. Wisdom, maturity, and self-mastery include recognizing both our strengths and our vulnerabilities. Humility means knowing our limits: none of us is invincible or limitless in power, and sometimes the wisest choice is to remove ourselves or simply run away from temptation.

Modern society's viewpoint of running away from a battle is seen as cowardly. But we have clearly seen in Joseph's example that his decision to run saved him. Less, he would have fallen. To apply earthly wisdom, the bible teaches us in Luke 14:31–32 that what king, going out to encounter another king in war, will not sit down first and deliberate whether he is able with ten thousand to meet him who comes against him with twenty thousand? Luke 14:28 says, “For which of you, desiring to build a tower, does not first sit down and count the cost, whether he has enough to complete it? This is wisdom at its finest. These two verses show us that to fully get the best results in life, you have to first pray about the situation by asking God for wisdom, understanding, and guidance, and then have the patience to wait on God's word instead of acting on impulse. Because it is not following these divine instructions is what leads to wrong decisions, losses, and even worse death.
