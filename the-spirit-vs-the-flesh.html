The Spirit vs. The Flesh

The Inner Conflict Between Desire and Obedience

By Jimey Grant Lafayette, Louisiana Second Watch

Galatians 5:17 says the flesh desires what is contrary to the Spirit, and the Spirit what is contrary to the flesh. Let's look at this scripture closely. Notice it has nothing to do with satan, demonic forces, entities, or influences. It says the flesh. Now, what is the flesh? The flesh is our mortal body. What makes up the flesh is the things that our body likes as well as our thoughts and emotions. For example, food, what we like to listen to, what we like to watch, and what entertains us. This is known as the lusts of the flesh. As Holy Spirit-filled believers in Christ, we are taught to crucify this flesh by fasting. Many other disciplines are used to tame our flesh, but the number 1 discipline is fasting. What fasting does is temporarily eliminate the fleshly desires of our earthly pleasures and cause us to focus more on our spiritual walk with Christ. In the process of fasting, you will feel the full effect of Galatians 5:17. That urge to wanna watch that specific show, that hunger to wanna eat that specific food, that desire to want to go to that routine favorite place that you like to go to but decide not to, that urge to wanna have that specific conversation when you know you should be engaging with the Lord.

This is where that urge if fought for a long amount of time turns into a type of fire. That spiritual feeling is the battle and crucifixion of your flesh. Now note, you don't have to be filled with the Holy Spirit to experience this feeling. You want to experience this feeling? Go without social media for 3 days, go without eating for 3 days, and if you are a smoker who is reading this, go without smoking for x amount of hours or days.

Paul taught us to crucify our flesh because the things of the flesh are what tune us out of the spiritual realm. You get so caught up in the indulgences of this world that you forget to read your bible, pray, study, and even fast. Life has its way of distracting us from these vital spiritual principles. The world has drugs, alcohol, sports, movies, various forms of entertainment, and food. Here is why self-control is the driving force. None of these worldly indulgences forces you to partake in them. It is simply your choice to either say yes or no. No demonic influence, no demonic spirits. It's your own human spirit,will, and choice. Now, what helps you overcome is the spiritual disciplines. But once again, with the spiritual disciplines comes self-control and the ability to say no to that movie and say yes to studying your word.

We have to be careful as Christians not to over-spiritualize certain situations and choices. We have to learn self-accountability. The ability to look in the mirror and to judge ourselves accordingly based on the certain decisions that we have made. No, satan did not make you eat 3 boxes of cakes. No, satan did not make you decide to go out with friends instead of studying your word. You made a choice, and you made a decision. I'm not saying that we cannot enjoy ourselves. But Ecclesiastes chapter 3 tells us there's a certain time and place for everything, and Proverbs 25:16 says, "If you find honey, eat just enough—too much of it, and you will vomit." We veer off when we start to put our indulgences above Christ. And our lives become more saturated with the pleasures of life rather than the things of God.

So as a Christian, I'm not saying that going out to eat, going to the movies, or enjoying yourself is wrong. Just make sure your indulgences are godly, align with the bible, and that we have enough self-control to put the PlayStation or Xbox controller down and focus on our bible and our walk with Christ.
