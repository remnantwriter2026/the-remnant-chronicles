When Trials Come, Grace Sustains

The Mystery of the Enduring Grace

By Jimey Grant Lafayette, Louisiana Second Watch

Have you ever been in a season in life when everything seems to be going wrong? It feels like one thing happens after another—one problem after the next. This can be indicators of two spiritual signs: either a trial from the Lord to test your faith, or a sign that satan is fighting against a significant breakthrough or blessing that has been manifested in your life. But what do we do as Christians during those times when it feels as though we are being attacked from all sides?

The Bible tells us in Proverbs 24:10, “If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small.” When we think of strength, most of us think of physical strength. But the Bible says that “we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against spiritual wickedness.”

It is in these intense times, or what the bible refers to as fiery trials in 1 Peter 4:12, that the Lord bestows grace upon us. When we think of grace, we often think of a free pass, a gift, or mercy from God. But let me show you another dimension of grace: enduring grace. This is a spiritual empowerment that helps you get through a difficult situation.

We can see this kind of supernatural empowerment in the lives of Elijah and Paul. When Elijah was tired, burned out, and exhausted in the wilderness, 1 Kings 19:5–8 tells us that an angel twice told him to get up and eat, because the journey was too great for him. Elijah was not immediately delivered from his trial, but he was given grace through the food the angel provided—food that symbolized sustenance and provision—so he could endure his trial.

We also see grace at work in Paul’s situation in 2 Corinthians 12:7–9. Paul says that because of his revelations, he was given a thorn in his flesh, “a messenger of Satan.” Three times, he pleaded with the Lord to take it away. But the Lord said, “My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.” As we see here, grace is not always an empowerment that ends a trial instantly; it can also be an empowerment that helps you endure it.

Notice that both Elijah and Paul were in communication with the Lord. So what does this teach us to do when we find ourselves in these seasons? Pray. Prayer and faith are what we can turn to, and when you pray, ask for the grace to endure.

Here is some encouragement and spiritual insight: Depending on the trial, your spiritual warfare may be happening because a breakthrough has already taken place spiritually and is being manifested naturally. We see an example of this in Daniel chapter 10. While Daniel fasted and prayed for 21 days, he encountered opposition from the prince of Persia. When Gabriel arrived with a message for Daniel, he explained that the prince of Persia had resisted him. This shows that God had already heard Daniel’s prayer and that the answer was being delivered through Gabriel. The opposition from the prince of Persia came as a response to the answer of Daniel’s prayer that was being delivered.

Remember that trials come in many shapes and forms and they come not to destroy us. But to shape, mold, try, and even reward us for our enduring faith, in the midst of the trial. In those times when the trial gets heated, pray, trust God, and ask Him for the enduring grace to make it through. The trial may not end immediately, but God can and will sustain you through it and strengthen you when you feel like you can't go on anymore. Keep standing in faith, knowing that your season of adversity does not have the final word.
